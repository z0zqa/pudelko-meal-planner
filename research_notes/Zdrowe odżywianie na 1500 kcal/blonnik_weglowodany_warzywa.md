# Błonnik, węglowodany, cukry wolne, warzywa i owoce: targets for a ~1500 kcal, lactose-free, low-cooking-time diet (healthy adult)

Research date: 2026-10-23. Primary sources used where reachable: WHO news releases (2015, 2023), EFSA opinions (2010, 2022), the NIZP-PZH "Normy żywienia dla populacji Polski" 2024, carbohydrate chapter (Przygoda, Wojtasik, Pietraś-Krzyżewska, Kunachowicz; full text read from the PDF), Harvard Nutrition Source. Some evidence items (Reynolds 2019, Aune 2017, DIETFITS, Miketinas 2019) were checked through abstracts/repositories and press summaries rather than full text; these are flagged.

## 1. Fiber: how much (EFSA, WHO 2023, Polish norms, Reynolds 2019, per 1000 kcal)

### Takeaway
Every relevant authority converges on **≥25 g/day** for adults: EFSA AI 25 g (2010), WHO 2023 ≥25 g of *naturally occurring* fiber, and the Polish 2024 norms AI 25 g/day for ages 19–65 (20 g for >65), unchanged from 2020. The Reynolds 2019 Lancet meta-analyses found the largest risk reductions at 25–29 g/day, with dose-response curves suggesting more is better. Because the target is absolute (not per kcal), a 1500 kcal diet needs a much higher fiber *density* (~17 g/1000 kcal) than a typical diet.

### Cited Findings
- EFSA (Scientific Opinion on DRVs for carbohydrates and dietary fibre, EFSA Journal 2010;8(3):1462): dietary fibre intakes of **25 g/day are adequate for normal laxation in adults**; fibre defined as non-digestible carbohydrates plus lignin; children 2 g/MJ. — [EFSA summary / opinion search results](https://efsa.europa.eu/en/supporting/pub/e15121); [FoodIngredientsFirst on EFSA DRVs](https://www.foodingredientsfirst.com/news/efsa-sets-european-dietary-reference-values-for-nutrient-intakes.html)
- The Polish 2024 norms note that the EFSA panel indicated that in adults **fibre intakes above 25 g/day may be beneficial** for weight maintenance and reducing risk of diet-related disease. — [NIZP-PZH, Normy żywienia 2024, rozdz. Węglowodany (PDF)](https://www.pzh.gov.pl/download/41598/?tmstv=1753861267)
- WHO carbohydrate guideline (published 17 July 2023): adults should consume **at least 25 g/day of naturally occurring dietary fibre**; children 2–5 y ≥15 g, 6–9 y ≥21 g, ≥10 y ≥25 g; carbohydrates should come primarily from whole grains, vegetables, fruits and pulses. — [WHO news release 17-07-2023](https://www.who.int/news/item/17-07-2023-who-updates-guidelines-on-fats-and-carbohydrates); [WHO guideline page, ISBN 9789240073593](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789240073593)
- "Naturally occurring" means fibre intrinsic to foods (not isolated/added fibre supplements). — [WHO news release](https://www.who.int/news/item/17-07-2023-who-updates-guidelines-on-fats-and-carbohydrates); [Healthline summary](https://www.healthline.com/health-news/what-the-who-says-about-how-much-carbs-fat-and-fiber-you-should-eat)
- **Polish norms 2024 (NIZP-PZH, published 31 Dec 2024)**: fibre set at **AI level**; men and women **19–65 y: 25 g/day**; 66–75 and >75 y: 20 g/day ("w indywidualnych przypadkach poziom zależny od wskazań lekarskich i dietetycznych"); pregnancy/lactation: to be set with a doctor/dietitian. Values based on EFSA and **unchanged vs. 2020 edition**. — [NIZP-PZH Normy 2024, Tabela 9 (PDF)](https://www.pzh.gov.pl/download/41598/?tmstv=1753861267); [pzh.gov.pl Normy żywienia 2024](https://www.pzh.gov.pl/normy-zywienia-2024/)
- Polish norms: no EAR/RDA can be set for fibre (insufficient data), hence AI; country recommendations range 18–38 g/day; France and Germany 30 g/day; UK FSA 18 g (old), UK SACN 2015 **30 g/day**; Nordic 2023 ≥25 g women / ≥35 g men; USA/Canada 25 g women / 38 g men; Japan 25/30 g. — [NIZP-PZH Normy 2024, Węglowodany (PDF)](https://www.pzh.gov.pl/download/41598/?tmstv=1753861267)
- Polish norms cite **ILSI: 10 g fibre per 1000 kcal** as an indicated diet density. — [NIZP-PZH Normy 2024, Węglowodany (PDF)](https://www.pzh.gov.pl/download/41598/?tmstv=1753861267)
- Polish norms: US HHS suggests ≥5–10 g/day **soluble** fibre, and 10–25 g/day to lower high LDL; no country sets separate soluble/insoluble targets. — [NIZP-PZH Normy 2024 (PDF)](https://www.pzh.gov.pl/download/41598/?tmstv=1753861267)
- Actual intake in Poland is below target: newer data show mean **17.5 g/day in women and 20.9 g/day in men**; older Szponar data 19.4–20 g women, 25–34 g men; cereal products supply ~54% of fibre in the Polish diet, vegetables + potatoes ~33%. — [NIZP-PZH Normy 2024 (PDF)](https://www.pzh.gov.pl/download/41598/?tmstv=1753861267)
- **Reynolds et al., Lancet 2019** (Carbohydrate quality and human health; WHO-commissioned): 185 prospective studies (~135 million person-years) and 58 clinical trials (4635 adults); highest vs lowest fibre consumers had **15–30% lower** all-cause and cardiovascular mortality, and lower incidence of CHD, stroke, type 2 diabetes and colorectal cancer; **risk reduction greatest at 25–29 g/day**; dose-response curves suggested **higher intakes could confer even greater benefit** for CVD, T2D, colorectal and breast cancer. Certainty: moderate for fibre, low–moderate for whole grains, low–very low for GI/GL. — [Reynolds et al. Lancet 2019, author manuscript (Univ. Dundee)](https://discovery.dundee.ac.uk/ws/files/30375889/Final_Lancet_for_John.pdf); [TCTMD summary](https://www.tctmd.com/news/dose-response-benefits-fiber-intake-seen-across-wide-swath-acquired-diseases)
- Reynolds 2019 also found similar findings for whole grains (and that GI/GL associations were weaker). — [Reynolds et al. Lancet 2019, manuscript](https://discovery.dundee.ac.uk/ws/files/30375889/Final_Lancet_for_John.pdf)

### Inferences
- **Per-energy math for 1500 kcal:** 25 g ÷ 1.5 = **~16.7 g/1000 kcal**; 30 g = 20 g/1000 kcal. This is well above the ILSI 10 g/1000 kcal density and above the US-style 14 g/1000 kcal figure (14 g × 1.5 = only 21 g, which would fall *below* the 25 g absolute AI). Recommendation for the app: use the **absolute target (≥25 g/day; stretch goal 30 g)**, not a per-kcal rule, during energy restriction.
- Suggested app targets: **minimum 25 g/day, optimal 25–35 g/day**; per meal for 3 meals + 1 snack: ~7–9 g per main meal, 3–5 g per snack (e.g. breakfast 8 g, lunch 9 g, dinner 7 g, snack 4 g ≈ 28 g).
- Because WHO specifies *naturally occurring* fibre, the app should count fibre from whole foods; fibre-enriched products/supplements can be shown but not used to "meet" the WHO target.

### Gaps
- The 14 g/1000 kcal figure (US IOM 2005 / Dietary Guidelines for Americans) was not re-verified in this session (DGA PDF exceeded fetch size); it is widely cited but should be confirmed before quoting.
- WHO 2023 recommendation strength (strong vs conditional) for the fibre and fruit/veg items was not confirmed from the full guideline text.
- Full-text Reynolds dose-response numbers (e.g. RR per 8 g/day increment) not extracted.

## 2. How to raise fiber practically and fast; content per portion; gradual increase, fluids; soluble vs insoluble; satiety/weight loss

### Takeaway
In Poland the most efficient sources are whole-grain rye bread, oat/barley flakes, buckwheat, legumes, dried fruit, nuts/seeds and vegetables (especially Brussels sprouts, peas, carrots), plus berries (raspberries, currants 6.4–7.8 g/100 g). Increase gradually with adequate fluid. Higher fibre intake predicted weight loss and diet adherence in a calorie-restricted trial (Miketinas 2019, POUNDS Lost).

### Cited Findings
- **Fibre per 100 g (Polish food composition, Kunachowicz et al., as reproduced in Normy 2024 Tabela 4):** wheat bran 42.4; almonds 12.9; barley flakes 9.6; dried apricots 10.3; dried plums (śliwki) 9.4; whole-grain rye bread 9.1; hazelnuts 8.9; brown rice (dry) 8.7; oat flakes 6.9; corn flakes 6.6; raisins 6.5; sunflower bread 6.4; sunflower seeds 6.0; buckwheat (dry) 5.9; carrot 3.6; "chleb baltonowski" 3.3; egg pasta (dry) 2.6; white rice (dry) 2.4; beetroot 2.2; apple 2.0; kaiser roll 1.9; strawberries 1.8; grapes 1.5; multivegetable juice 1.2; orange juice 0.1. Dishes per 100 g: boiled Brussels sprouts 4.9; fasola po bretońsku 4.0; green pea soup 3.5; carrot-apple salad 2.9; cooked buckwheat 2.1; chicken in vegetables 1.6; boiled potatoes 1.4; cooked white rice 0.8; cooked egg pasta 0.8. — [NIZP-PZH Normy 2024, Węglowodany, Tabela 4 (PDF)](https://www.pzh.gov.pl/download/41598/?tmstv=1753861267)
- **Soluble/insoluble per 100 g (Tabela 5):** dried peas 15.0 total (4.6 sol./10.4 insol.); green peas 6.0 (0.6/5.4); carrot 3.6 (1.7/1.9); eggplant 2.5 (1.2/1.3); Chinese cabbage 1.9 (1.0/0.9); banana 1.7 (0.7/1.0); apple 2.0 (0.5/1.5); orange 1.9 (1.2/0.7); dried apples 10.3 (2.6/7.7); wheat flour type 500 2.3; buckwheat 5.9 (0.8/5.1); wholemeal rye bread 8.4 (2.0/6.4); "chleb mazowiecki" 3.2. — [NIZP-PZH Normy 2024, Tabela 5 (PDF)](https://www.pzh.gov.pl/download/41598/?tmstv=1753861267)
- Vegetables range 0.5–5.8 g/100 g; fruit average ~2 g/100 g; exceptions **raspberries and currants 6.4–7.8 g/100 g**; legumes are the richest vegetable group; dishes 0–4.9 g/100 g, so fibre must come from many foods. — [NIZP-PZH Normy 2024 (PDF)](https://www.pzh.gov.pl/download/41598/?tmstv=1753861267)
- Good **soluble** fibre sources: oats, barley, fruit (apples, citrus), vegetables (parsley, carrot, eggplant), legumes (peas, beans), flaxseed, psyllium, nuts. Insoluble fibre is mainly in whole-grain cereal products. Soluble (viscous) fractions slow gastric emptying and glucose absorption, lower cholesterol (bile-acid binding) and are fermented to SCFA; insoluble fibre increases stool bulk and speeds transit. — [NIZP-PZH Normy 2024 (PDF)](https://www.pzh.gov.pl/download/41598/?tmstv=1753861267)
- EU claim thresholds: "source of fibre" = **≥3 g/100 g** (high fibre = 6 g/100 g per EU Reg. 1924/2006; Normy Tabela 6 lists the 3 g criterion). — [NIZP-PZH Normy 2024, Tabela 6 (PDF)](https://www.pzh.gov.pl/download/41598/?tmstv=1753861267)
- **Excess/rapid increase:** too much fibre can cause bloating, diarrhoea or even bowel obstruction **in people who do not drink enough fluids**; may lower absorption of minerals (Ca, Fe) and fat-soluble vitamins at very high intakes; restricted in Crohn's disease/obstruction. Bran/fibre supplements may be used in adults for obesity prevention per doctor/dietitian advice. — [NIZP-PZH Normy 2024 (PDF)](https://www.pzh.gov.pl/download/41598/?tmstv=1753861267)
- **Satiety/weight loss — Miketinas et al., J Nutr 2019;149(10):1742–1748 (POUNDS Lost):** 345 adults on 4 energy-restricted diets (−750 kcal/day); **fibre intake was the strongest predictor of weight loss**, independent of macronutrient composition; effect similar across diet types; participants meeting fibre goals adhered better. — [PMC6768815](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6768815/); [ASN summary](https://nutrition.org/dietary-fiber-an-integral-component-of-a-successful-weight-loss-intervention/)
- In POUNDS Lost, subjects with prediabetes and low fasting insulin lost 5.6 kg more with ≥35 g vs <35 g fibre/10 MJ (Hjorth et al., Nutrients 2019; small subgroups, n=15 vs 16). — [Hjorth et al. 2019](https://test01.ku.dk/:obvius/pureproxy/3974268/en/publications/pretreatment-fasting-glucose-and-insulin-as-determinants-of-weigh)
- **Viscous fibre meta-analyses (Jovanovski et al.):** AJCN 2020;111(2):471–485 on viscous fibre with ad libitum diets (reported modest ~0.33 kg weight reduction at median ~8 g/day — secondary summary only); companion Eur J Nutr 2021 meta-analysis in calorie-restricted diets: **−0.81 kg vs control (15 studies, n=1347)**. — [PubMed 32198674 (AJCN 2020)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32198674/); [Springer Medicine (Eur J Nutr 2021)](https://www.springermedicine.com/obesity/obesity/effect-of-viscous-fiber-supplementation-on-obesity-indicators-in/20942650)
- Polish norms: low-fibre diets are typically high in energy density and contribute to obesity. — [NIZP-PZH Normy 2024 (PDF)](https://www.pzh.gov.pl/download/41598/?tmstv=1753861267)

### Inferences
- **Fibre per typical portion (computed from the Polish per-100 g values above; portion sizes are assumptions):**
  - 2 slices whole-grain rye bread (~70 g): ~6.4 g (vs 2 kaiser rolls 100 g: ~1.9 g)
  - Oat flakes 50 g (dry): ~3.5 g; barley flakes 50 g: ~4.8 g
  - Buckwheat 70 g dry (≈ one cooked portion): ~4.1 g; brown rice 70 g dry: ~6.1 g vs white rice 70 g dry: ~1.7 g
  - Dried peas/legumes 60 g dry: ~9 g; green peas (frozen) 100 g: 6 g; fasola po bretońsku 250 g: ~10 g
  - Brussels sprouts (frozen, boiled) 150 g: ~7.4 g; carrot 100 g: 3.6 g; beetroot 150 g: ~3.3 g
  - Raspberries/currants 100 g (frozen OK): 6.4–7.8 g; apple 180 g: ~3.6 g; banana 120 g: ~2 g
  - Almonds 25 g: ~3.2 g; sunflower seeds 15 g: ~0.9 g; dried plums 30 g: ~2.8 g; wheat bran 10 g (1 tbsp-ish): ~4.2 g
- Quick-swaps with the biggest gain for little cooking time: white roll → whole-grain rye bread (+~4 g per 2 slices); white rice → buckwheat/brown rice (+2–4 g); add a handful of frozen berries to oats (+5–7 g); add a 150 g bag of frozen Brussels sprouts/peas/vegetable mix to dinner (+5–9 g); canned (rinsed) beans/lentils/chickpeas in salads/soups (+5–9 g per half can, estimate).
- Example ~27–30 g day at 1500 kcal (illustrative): oats 50 g + 100 g frozen raspberries (~10 g) / 2 slices rye bread + vegetables (~8 g) / buckwheat 60 g + 150 g frozen broccoli/sprouts (~10 g) / apple (~3.5 g).
- App logic: when a user's baseline is low (Polish women average 17.5 g), raise ~3–5 g/day per week (common clinical practice; not a specific source number — see Gaps) and pair with a fluid reminder.
- Lactose-free relevance: lactose is ~5% of cow's milk; dairy contributes no fibre, so lactose-free substitution doesn't affect fibre; plant "milks" vary.

### Gaps
- No authoritative source found in this session giving a specific weekly ramp-up rate (g/week) or a specific fluid amount per gram of fibre; Polish norms only state that adequate fluids are needed.
- Fibre per portion for canned legumes (cooked weight) and Polish-specific portion standards (e.g., "porcja" sizes from IŻŻ Album fotografii) not retrieved; above portion values are computations.
- The Jovanovski AJCN 2020 numbers come from a secondary summary.

## 3. Carbohydrates: reference range, quality, glycemic considerations, low-carb vs higher-carb for weight loss

### Takeaway
EFSA: 45–60% energy; Polish norms 2024: **45–65% energy** (RI, unchanged from 2017), with added/free sugars ≤10%; WHO 2023 did not change the 40–70% range but focused on quality (whole grains, vegetables, fruit, pulses). For weight loss, healthy low-carb and healthy low-fat produce similar results (DIETFITS); quality matters more than the ratio.

### Cited Findings
- EFSA 2010: **45–60 E%** reference intake range for carbohydrates for adults and children >1 y; insufficient data to set an upper limit for (added) sugars; evidence on glycaemic index/load for weight maintenance and disease prevention inconclusive. — [EFSA DRV summary](https://efsa.europa.eu/en/supporting/pub/e15121); [FoodIngredientsFirst](https://www.foodingredientsfirst.com/news/efsa-sets-european-dietary-reference-values-for-nutrient-intakes.html)
- **Polish norms 2024:** carbohydrates (incl. fibre) set as RI = **45–65% of daily energy** for all groups ≥1 y (values from IŻŻ 2017 retained "due to lack of new epidemiological data"), **of which added/free sugars no more than 10% of energy**. — [NIZP-PZH Normy 2024, Tabela 8 (PDF)](https://www.pzh.gov.pl/download/41598/?tmstv=1753861267)
- Polish norms: 50–100 g carbohydrate/day prevents ketosis; **~130 g/day is estimated to cover the brain's glucose needs** (not a full energy requirement); very-low-carb diets risk ketosis, micronutrient deficiencies (B vitamins, A, E, C, Zn, Cu, Se), and with excess protein, renal risk. — [NIZP-PZH Normy 2024 (PDF)](https://www.pzh.gov.pl/download/41598/?tmstv=1753861267)
- Polish norms on GI/GL: GL (GI × available carbs ÷ 100) is the better indicator of real glycaemic effect; long-term high-GI/GL diets are linked to CHD, T2D, NAFLD, gout and cancers. — [NIZP-PZH Normy 2024 (PDF)](https://www.pzh.gov.pl/download/41598/?tmstv=1753861267)
- WHO 2023: carbohydrate intake should come primarily from whole grains, vegetables, fruits and pulses; the update did not revise total carbohydrate amount (WHO's general range 40–70% energy). — [WHO news release](https://www.who.int/news/item/17-07-2023-who-updates-guidelines-on-fats-and-carbohydrates); [Harvard Nutrition Source on WHO 2023](https://nutritionsource.hsph.harvard.edu/2023/07/17/who-updated-guidelines-healthy-diets-total-fat/)
- Reynolds 2019: evidence for fibre (moderate) and whole grains (low–moderate) much stronger than for GI/GL (low–very low). — [Reynolds et al. Lancet 2019 manuscript](https://discovery.dundee.ac.uk/ws/files/30375889/Final_Lancet_for_John.pdf)
- **DIETFITS (Gardner et al., JAMA 2018;319(7):667–679):** 609 adults randomised to healthy low-fat vs healthy low-carb for 12 months (481 completed); weight loss ~5.3 kg vs ~6.0 kg, **no significant difference**; neither genotype pattern nor baseline insulin secretion modified the effect; LDL rose in the low-carb arm. Both arms emphasised whole, minimally processed foods, vegetables, and minimal added sugar/refined flour. — [ScienceDaily (Stanford release)](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2018/02/180220123124.htm); [NIHR Evidence summary](https://evidence.nihr.ac.uk/alert/low-fat-or-low-carbohydrate-diets-seem-just-as-effective-for-weight-loss/)
- Miketinas 2019: fibre–weight-loss association did not differ across macronutrient diet types. — [PMC6768815](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6768815/)

### Inferences
- **Grams at 1500 kcal (4 kcal/g):** 45% = 169 g; 50% = 188 g; 55% = 206 g; 60% = 225 g; 65% = 244 g. A sensible app default during energy restriction (with protein raised for satiety/lean mass) is **~45–50% (≈170–190 g/day)**, never below the ~130 g brain-glucose estimate unless chosen deliberately.
- Per meal (3 meals + snack): ~45–55 g carbs per main meal, 15–25 g per snack.
- Quality rule for the app: ≥ half of grain servings whole-grain; favour low-GL combos (grain + legumes/veg + protein + fat) rather than optimising GI per food.

### Gaps
- The WHO 2023 guideline's exact wording on total carbohydrate % was not read from the full PDF.
- No head-to-head trial at exactly 1500 kcal found; DIETFITS was ad libitum with reduction coaching, not fixed kcal.

## 4. Free/added sugars: WHO, EFSA 2022, Polish norms; grams at 1500 kcal

### Takeaway
WHO: free sugars <10% of energy (strong), further to <5% (conditional). EFSA 2022: no safe level/UL could be set; intake should be "as low as possible" within a nutritionally adequate diet. Polish norms 2024: added/free sugars ≤10% energy. At 1500 kcal: <37.5 g/day (<10%), ideally <19 g/day (<5%).

### Cited Findings
- **WHO Guideline: Sugars intake for adults and children (4 March 2015):** reduce free sugars to **<10% of total energy** (strong recommendation); further reduction to **<5% (~25 g / 6 teaspoons per day at 2000 kcal)** for additional benefits (conditional recommendation). Free sugars = mono- and disaccharides added by manufacturer/cook/consumer plus sugars naturally present in honey, syrups, fruit juices and fruit juice concentrates. — [WHO news release 04-03-2015](https://who.int/news/item/04-03-2015-who-calls-on-countries-to-reduce-sugars-intake-among-adults-and-children)
- The <5% recommendation's evidence base was low quality (dental caries ecological data incl. wartime rationing), hence conditional. — [Foodwatch summary of WHO 2015](https://www.foodwatch.com.au/?p=10661)
- **EFSA 2022 (EFSA Journal 2022;20(2):7074, adopted 15 Dec 2021, published 28 Feb 2022):** no UL or safe intake level could be set for total/added/free sugars; risk of chronic metabolic diseases and dental caries increases in a dose-dependent manner across the observed range; intake of added and free sugars should be **"as low as possible in the context of a nutritionally adequate diet"**. — [EFSA opinion PDF](https://www.efsa.europa.eu/sites/default/files/2022-02/efs2_7074_0.pdf); [FoodNavigator](https://www.foodnavigator.com/Article/2022/03/03/intakes-of-added-and-free-sugars-should-be-as-low-as-possible-as-part-of-a-nutritionally-adequate-diet-efsa/)
- A critique (Löwik et al.) argues the "as low as possible" conclusion rests mainly on surrogate endpoints, and that trials' lowest intakes (~10 E%) showed no adverse effects. — [PMC8884083](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8884083)
- **Polish norms 2024:** added/free sugars ≤10% of energy for adults; for children ≥2 y and adolescents free sugars <5%; the chapter restates EFSA 2022 ("konieczność możliwie jak najniższego spożycia cukrów dodanych i wolnych"). — [NIZP-PZH Normy 2024 (PDF)](https://www.pzh.gov.pl/download/41598/?tmstv=1753861267)
- Harvard Healthy Eating Plate: skip sugary drinks; drink water, coffee or tea. — [Harvard Nutrition Source](https://nutritionsource.hsph.harvard.edu/healthy-eating-plate/)

### Inferences
- **At 1500 kcal (4 kcal/g):** 10% = **37.5 g/day** (~9 tsp); 5% = **18.75 g/day** (~4–5 tsp). App: hard cap 37 g, target ≤19 g; per meal ≤5–6 g free sugars as default.
- Fruit juice (even 100%) and honey count as free sugars; whole fruit does not. Lactose-free dairy: lactose is hydrolysed to glucose+galactose in lactose-free milk — these are intrinsic milk sugars, not free sugars per WHO definition (WHO excludes sugars naturally in milk), though they taste sweeter. Flavoured lactose-free yoghurts contain added sugar.

### Gaps
- WHO's explicit statement that sugars from lactose-free (enzymatically hydrolysed) milk are not "free sugars" was not verified; inference from the definition.

## 5. Vegetables and fruit: WHO ≥400 g, Polish "Talerz zdrowego żywienia", Aune 2017 (~800 g), frozen/canned, portions

### Takeaway
WHO: ≥400 g/day (adults) of vegetables and fruit (potatoes excluded). Polish plate (NIZP-PZH, 2020): half the plate vegetables and fruit, with ¾ vegetables and ¼ fruit. Aune 2017 found risk reductions continuing up to ~800 g/day. For a 1500 kcal diet, high-volume, low-energy vegetables are the key tool.

### Cited Findings
- WHO 2023: adults ≥**400 g/day** vegetables and fruit; children 2–5 y ≥250 g, 6–9 y ≥350 g, ≥10 y ≥400 g. — [WHO news release 17-07-2023](https://www.who.int/news/item/17-07-2023-who-updates-guidelines-on-fats-and-carbohydrates)
- **"Talerz zdrowego żywienia" (NIZP-PZH, 2020, replaced the pyramid for adults):** ½ plate vegetables and fruit (more vegetables than fruit, proportion **¾ vegetables : ¼ fruit**, minimum 400 g/day), ¼ whole-grain cereal products, ¼ protein foods. — [mp.pl: Koniec z piramidą żywienia](https://www.mp.pl/pacjent/dieta/aktualnosci/251137,koniec-z-piramida-zywienia-zalecenia-podane-na-talerzu); [pacjent.gov.pl](https://pacjent.gov.pl/print/pdf/node/958)
- **Aune et al., Int J Epidemiol 2017 (95 studies, 142 publications):** per 200 g/day fruit+veg RR all-cause mortality **0.90 (0.87–0.93)**; risk reductions observed **up to 800 g/day** for all outcomes except cancer (600 g/day); ~31% lower all-cause mortality at 800 g vs none; if causal, **5.6 and 7.8 million premature deaths** in 2013 attributable to intakes below 500 and 800 g/day. Observational evidence. — [White Rose eprint](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/106013/); [ScienceDaily](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2017/02/170223114807.htm)
- Harvard Healthy Eating Plate: ½ plate vegetables and fruits (aim for colour variety); **potatoes and French fries don't count as vegetables**. — [Harvard Nutrition Source](https://nutritionsource.hsph.harvard.edu/healthy-eating-plate/)
- Fruit fibre ~2 g/100 g average; vegetables 0.5–5.8 g/100 g; raspberries/currants 6.4–7.8 g/100 g (frozen berries are a practical year-round source). — [NIZP-PZH Normy 2024 (PDF)](https://www.pzh.gov.pl/download/41598/?tmstv=1753861267)
- Fruit juice counts as free sugar (WHO 2015) and multivegetable juice has 1.2 g fibre/100 g vs orange juice 0.1 g. — [WHO 2015](https://who.int/news/item/04-03-2015-who-calls-on-countries-to-reduce-sugars-intake-among-adults-and-children); [NIZP-PZH Normy 2024 (PDF)](https://www.pzh.gov.pl/download/41598/?tmstv=1753861267)

### Inferences
- App targets: **minimum 400 g/day, goal 500–600 g, stretch 800 g**; split ~¾ veg / ¼ fruit → e.g. 450 g veg + 150–200 g fruit (~1–2 fruits). Per main meal: ≥150 g vegetables (half the plate); fruit as breakfast topping/snack.
- Energy cost: 400–600 g mostly non-starchy veg + 1–2 fruit ≈ 150–250 kcal (estimate), i.e. ~10–15% of 1500 kcal, while supplying ~10–15 g fibre.
- Low-time options: frozen vegetable mixes, frozen berries, pre-washed salads, cherry tomatoes, baby carrots, canned tomatoes, canned legumes (rinse to reduce salt), sauerkraut/pickles (salt). Count potatoes as starch, not vegetables (Harvard); Polish plate treats them with cereal/starch foods (verify).
- Portion heuristic: 1 portion ≈ 80–100 g (common convention; not verified from a Polish primary source here).

## 6. Whole grains (Harvard Healthy Eating Plate and others)

### Takeaway
Harvard recommends ¼ of the plate as whole grains and limiting refined grains; the Polish plate likewise assigns ¼ to (preferably whole-grain) cereal products; Reynolds 2019 found whole-grain benefits similar to fibre (low–moderate certainty).

### Cited Findings
- Harvard Healthy Eating Plate: "Go for whole grains – ¼ of your plate" (whole wheat, barley, quinoa, oats, brown rice); limit refined grains (white rice, white bread). — [Harvard Nutrition Source](https://nutritionsource.hsph.harvard.edu/healthy-eating-plate/)
- Polish plate: ¼ plate cereal products, whole-grain products described as more valuable. — [mp.pl](https://www.mp.pl/pacjent/dieta/aktualnosci/251137,koniec-z-piramida-zywienia-zalecenia-podane-na-talerzu)
- Polish norms: best cereal fibre sources are wholemeal rye bread, mixed bread with seeds, flakes (oat/barley); whole-grain rye bread 9.1 g fibre/100 g vs kaiser roll 1.9 g; brown rice 8.7 vs white rice 2.4 g/100 g dry. — [NIZP-PZH Normy 2024 (PDF)](https://www.pzh.gov.pl/download/41598/?tmstv=1753861267)
- Reynolds 2019: similar inverse associations for whole grain intake with mortality and NCD outcomes. — [Reynolds et al. Lancet 2019 manuscript](https://discovery.dundee.ac.uk/ws/files/30375889/Final_Lancet_for_John.pdf)

### Inferences
- App rule: default every grain choice to whole-grain (rye wholemeal bread, oats, buckwheat, barley groats, brown rice, whole-wheat pasta); flag refined grains. At 1500 kcal, ~3–4 grain servings/day (one per meal) fits the ¼-plate rule.

### Gaps
- A specific g/day whole-grain target (e.g. Reynolds' 50–75 g/day or a "≥48 g" US figure) was not verified in this session.
