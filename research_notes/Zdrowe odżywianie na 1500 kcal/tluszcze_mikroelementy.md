## 1. Fats: reference intakes and grams at 1500 kcal

### Takeaway
At 1500 kcal, total fat is about **33–58 g/day**: 20–35% of energy per EFSA and the PL norms, and ≤50 g at WHO's ≤30% limit. Saturated fat should stay **<16.7 g** (WHO <10% E). The stricter PL-norm target of 5–6% E works out to **about 8–10 g**. Trans fat should stay **<1.7 g** (WHO <1% E) and be as low as possible. Intake should include **250 mg EPA+DHA/day**, which is about 2 fish servings a week with 1 of them oily. Other targets are **ALA ≈0.8 g** (0.5% E) and **LA ≈6.7 g** (4% E).

### Cited Findings
- EFSA reference intake (RI) for total fat in adults ≥18 y is **20–35% of energy (E)**. SFA and trans fat (TFA) are "as low as possible" (ALAP). Linoleic acid (LA) AI is **4% E**, ALA AI is **0.5% E** and EPA+DHA AI is **250 mg/day**. Pregnancy and lactation add +100–200 mg DHA — [EFSA DRV Summary Report, Table 1](https://www.efsa.europa.eu/sites/default/files/2017_09_DRVs_summary_report.pdf)
- EFSA states that 250 mg/day EPA+DHA "appears to be sufficient" for primary prevention of cardiovascular disease and corresponds to "1 to 2 fatty fish meals per week" — [EFSA DRV Summary Report](https://www.efsa.europa.eu/sites/default/files/2017_09_DRVs_summary_report.pdf)
- PL norms 2020 set the fat RI for adults >18 y as the gram equivalent of **30% (range 20–35%) of energy** — [PL norms 2020, Ch. Tłuszcze (Mojska et al.)](https://ncez.pzh.gov.pl/wp-content/uploads/2021/03/normy_zywienia_2020web.pdf)
- PL norms 2020, Table 15 (AI for n-3/n-6) for adults: **ALA 0.5% E; DHA+EPA: 2 servings of fish/week, including 1 oily fish, or 250 mg/day; LA 4% E** — [PL norms 2020](https://ncez.pzh.gov.pl/wp-content/uploads/2021/03/normy_zywienia_2020web.pdf)
- PL norms 2020, Table 10/14 gives the **recommended maximum SFA for adults as 5–6% of energy**. In g/day this is 14.5–17.3 g (women 19–30), 14.0–16.8 g (women 31–50) and 18.3–22.0 g (men 19–30), all calculated at full-energy reference intakes. For children 1–9 y the limit is 10% E — [PL norms 2020](https://ncez.pzh.gov.pl/wp-content/uploads/2021/03/normy_zywienia_2020web.pdf)
- PL norms text: SFA intake should be "as low as possible" while keeping the diet nutritionally adequate. Trans-fat intake should be "as low as possible", and there are insufficient data to distinguish natural from industrial trans fats. Cutting total fat below the recommended range is *not* recommended — [PL norms 2020](https://ncez.pzh.gov.pl/wp-content/uploads/2021/03/normy_zywienia_2020web.pdf)
- WHO 2023 guideline: SFA should be **≤10% of total energy** and TFA **<1% of total energy** (industrial and ruminant combined), for everyone aged ≥2 y. SFA/TFA should be replaced with PUFA, plant-source MUFA, or carbohydrates from foods with naturally occurring fibre. Fat should be primarily unsaturated. A separate 2023 WHO total-fat guideline gives adult total fat **≤30% E**. The guideline replaces earlier WHO 1989/2002 guidance — [WHO guideline summary](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789240083592); [WHO news, 17 July 2023](https://www.who.int/news/item//17-07-2023-who-updates-guidelines-on-fats-and-carbohydrates)
- WHO-listed SFA sources: fatty meat, dairy, butter, ghee, lard, palm oil and coconut oil. TFA sources: baked and fried foods, pre-packaged snacks, and ruminant meat/dairy — [WHO news 2023](https://www.who.int/news/item//17-07-2023-who-updates-guidelines-on-fats-and-carbohydrates)
- PL norms note SFA share: coconut oil >80%, palm oil >40%. They cite ESC/AHA advice to replace SFA-rich foods (animal fats, tropical oils) with MUFA/PUFA sources. They also cite WHO's REPLACE plan to eliminate partially hydrogenated oils by 2023 — [PL norms 2020](https://ncez.pzh.gov.pl/wp-content/uploads/2021/03/normy_zywienia_2020web.pdf)
- PL norms list oily fish to eat weekly: Atlantic salmon, mackerel, sprat, herring and sardine — [PL norms 2020, Ch. Tłuszcze](https://ncez.pzh.gov.pl/wp-content/uploads/2021/03/normy_zywienia_2020web.pdf)

### Inferences
- Gram conversions at 1500 kcal (9 kcal/g fat):
  | Target | % E | g/day at 1500 kcal |
  |---|---|---|
  | Total fat (EFSA/PL range) | 20–35% | **33–58 g** (30% = **50 g**) |
  | Total fat (WHO upper) | ≤30% | **≤50 g** |
  | SFA (WHO 2023) | <10% | **<16.7 g** |
  | SFA (PL norms 2020 stricter) | 5–6% | **8.3–10 g** |
  | TFA (WHO) | <1% | **<1.7 g** (aim ≈0) |
  | LA (AI) | 4% | **≈6.7 g** |
  | ALA (AI) | 0.5% | **≈0.83 g** (e.g., ~1 tbsp rapeseed oil or 1 tsp ground flax — composition estimate, not from a cited source) |
  | EPA+DHA | — | **250 mg/day** (≈1.75 g/week) |
- App suggestion: use a default fat target of 50 g (30% E), flag below 33 g or above 58 g, warn when SFA exceeds 16.7 g, and show an "optimal" band of ≤10 g (PL 5–6%). Add a weekly fish counter with a goal of 2 servings, at least 1 oily. Below the 20% E floor, absorption of fat-soluble vitamins and essential fatty-acid intake become a concern, and the PL norms explicitly advise against going below the recommended range.
- For quick, low-effort meals: canned sardines, mackerel, herring or salmon; rapeseed/olive oil; nuts and seeds; avocado. Lean on these and limit butter, coconut/palm-oil products and fatty processed meat.

### Gaps
- I did not fetch the Harvard Nutrition Source fats page or specific EPA+DHA amounts per fish serving. Per-food omega-3 numbers need a food-composition source, such as the Polish IŻŻ tables (Kunachowicz et al., 2017).
- The PL SFA table uses % E of full-energy reference values. The 5–6% figure is the stricter PL norm. The WHO limit is <10%. The app should choose which one to use as the hard warning.

## 2. Micronutrients at risk on ~1500 kcal and with limited dairy: reference values (women vs men)

### Takeaway
The nutrients to track are **calcium, vitamin D, iodine, iron (women), B12 (if animal food is low), folate, magnesium and zinc**. The PL 2020 RDAs for adults 19–50 are:
- **Ca 1000 mg** (1200 mg for women ≥51 and men ≥66)
- **Fe 18 mg for women vs 10 mg for men** (10 mg for women ≥51)
- **I 150 µg**
- **Mg 310–320 mg for women, 400–420 mg for men**
- **Zn 8 mg for women, 11 mg for men**
- **Folate 400 µg DFE**
- **B12 2.4 µg**
- **Vitamin D AI 15 µg**

EFSA values differ slightly. Its calcium PRI is 950 mg (≥25 y), iron 16 mg for premenopausal women vs 11 mg for men, B12 AI 4 µg and folate 330 µg DFE.

### Cited Findings
**PL norms 2020 (NIZP-PZH), adult RDA unless noted (AI = adequate intake)** — [PL norms 2020, Tables 18–23](https://ncez.pzh.gov.pl/wp-content/uploads/2021/03/normy_zywienia_2020web.pdf):

| Nutrient | Women 19–30 | Women 31–50 | Women 51–65 | Men 19–30 | Men 31–50 | Men 51–65 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Calcium (mg) RDA (EAR) | 1000 (800) | 1000 (800) | **1200** (1000) | 1000 (800) | 1000 (800) | 1000 (800); 1200 from 66 y |
| Iron (mg) RDA (EAR) | **18** (8) | **18** (8) | 10 (6) | 10 (6) | 10 (6) | 10 (6) |
| Magnesium (mg) RDA | 310 | 320 | 320 | 400 | 420 | 420 |
| Zinc (mg) RDA (EAR) | 8 (6.8) | 8 | 8 | 11 (9.4) | 11 | 11 |
| Iodine (µg) RDA (EAR) | 150 (95) | 150 | 150 | 150 (95) | 150 | 150 |
| Folate (µg DFE) RDA (EAR) | 400 (320) | 400 | 400 | 400 (320) | 400 | 400 |
| Vitamin B12 (µg) RDA (EAR) | 2.4 (2.0) | 2.4 | 2.4 | 2.4 (2.0) | 2.4 | 2.4 |
| Vitamin D (µg) AI | 15 | 15 | 15 | 15 | 15 | 15 |
| Vitamin C (mg) RDA | 75 | 75 | 75 | 90 | 90 | 90 |
| Vitamin A (µg RAE) RDA | 700 | 700 | 700 | 900 | 900 | 900 |

**EFSA DRVs (adults)** — [EFSA DRV Summary Report, Tables 5, 7, 9, 11](https://www.efsa.europa.eu/sites/default/files/2017_09_DRVs_summary_report.pdf):
- Calcium PRI: **1000 mg** (18–24 y), **950 mg** (≥25 y), same for both sexes.
- Iron PRI: **men 11 mg; premenopausal women 16 mg; postmenopausal women 11 mg**.
- Iodine AI: **150 µg**.
- Magnesium AI: **men 350 mg, women 300 mg**.
- Zinc PRI depends on phytate intake (300/600/900/1200 mg/day phytate): **women 7.5/9.3/11.0/12.7 mg; men 9.4/11.7/14.0/16.3 mg**.
- Folate PRI: **330 µg DFE**.
- Vitamin B12 (cobalamin) AI: **4 µg/day** (4.5 µg in pregnancy).
- Vitamin C PRI: **men 110 mg, women 95 mg**.

**Food-source facts**
- **Iodine (PL)**: the main sources are seafood (especially cod and halibut, less so Baltic herring), **milk and dairy, eggs and iodized salt**. Poland has a population-level iodine-deficiency problem and made **salt iodization mandatory in 1997** — [PL norms 2020, Ch. Jod](https://ncez.pzh.gov.pl/wp-content/uploads/2021/03/normy_zywienia_2020web.pdf)
- Salt for direct consumption in Poland must contain **2.3 (±0.77) mg iodine per 100 g**, i.e. 30 ±10 mg KI or 39 ±13 mg KIO3 per kg (Rozporządzenie MZ of 13 March 2024, Dz.U. 2024 poz. 420). Salt used in food processing is not covered by the requirement — [Sejm interpellation no. 6044, MZ reply](https://api.sejm.gov.pl/sejm/term10/interpellations/6044/body); [mp.pl](https://www.mp.pl/pacjent/dieta/lista/201205,czy-znaczace-ograniczenie-soli-w-diecie-moze-powodowac-niedobor-jodu) (fetch blocked; snippet only)
- Plant drinks: in 309 unflavoured European plant-based dairy alternatives, **organic products had no added micronutrients** because EU law restricts fortifying organic foods. Among non-organic drinks, **76% were fortified with calcium, 66% with vitamin D and 60% with B12** — [Medici, Craig & Rowland, *Nutrients* 2023](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10421432/)
- A German consumer-centre market check found most conventional plant drinks calcium-fortified, nearly all at **120 mg Ca/100 ml** (≈ cow's milk). **71% had B12 added but only 29% had iodine** — [Verbraucherzentrale](https://www.verbraucherzentrale.de/wissen/lebensmittel/kennzeichnung-und-inhaltsstoffe/hafer-kokos-mandel-reis-soja-milchalternativen-unter-der-lupe-62593)
- UK SACN/COT: as of early 2022, no almond, oat or soy drink on the UK market was nutritionally equivalent to cow's milk — [NutritionInsight report](https://www.nutritioninsight.com/news/sacn-cot-plant-based-milk-nutritional-gaps-childrens-health.html)
- PL norms: soy, oat and rice drinks fortified with B12 can be a B12 source. Spirulina is *not* a reliable B12 source, because about 80% of its B12 is inactive pseudo-B12 — [PL norms 2020, Ch. Witamina B12](https://ncez.pzh.gov.pl/wp-content/uploads/2021/03/normy_zywienia_2020web.pdf)
- Calcium bioavailability (Harvard): milk ≈300 mg/cup with ~30% absorbed (~100 mg). **Cooked spinach has 260 mg/cup but only ~5% is absorbed (~13 mg)** because of oxalate. Cooked bok choy has 160 mg/cup with ~50% absorbed (~80 mg). **Calcium-set tofu and Ca-fortified juice have total calcium and bioavailability similar to milk.** Other sources listed: canned sardines/salmon with bones, almonds (~20% absorbed), kale, collards and edamame. Harvard's Healthy Eating Plate advises limiting milk/dairy to 1–2 servings/day — [Harvard T.H. Chan Nutrition Source: Calcium](https://nutritionsource.hsph.harvard.edu/calcium/)
- Iron: vitamin C reduces Fe(III) to Fe(II) and enhances absorption of non-heme iron. Non-heme iron (mainly plant foods) is less available than heme iron (animal foods), and its absorption is inhibited by phytates, fibre, oxalates and calcium — [PL norms 2020, Ch. Witamina C and Żelazo](https://ncez.pzh.gov.pl/wp-content/uploads/2021/03/normy_zywienia_2020web.pdf)
- Iodine absorption: about 90% of food iodine is absorbed. Goitrogens (in cabbage, cauliflower, peanuts) and soy flour, nitrates, fluorides, calcium, magnesium and iron can reduce iodine utilisation — [PL norms 2020](https://ncez.pzh.gov.pl/wp-content/uploads/2021/03/normy_zywienia_2020web.pdf)

### Inferences
- The **women 19–50 iron RDA of 18 mg** is the hardest target at 1500 kcal: about 12 mg Fe per 1000 kcal. Typical mixed diets supply roughly 6–7 mg/1000 kcal (general knowledge, not from a cited source), so the app should flag iron for menstruating women and pair plant iron with vitamin C at the same meal.
- **Calcium at 1000 mg with no regular milk.** Three fortified-drink or lactose-free dairy servings (~250 ml × 120 mg/100 ml ≈ 300 mg each) plus hard cheese or calcium-set tofu gets close to target. The app should **warn when the selected plant drink is "bio/organic"**, because these are unfortified. It should also not count spinach toward calcium at face value.
- **Iodine** risk goes up when dairy is replaced by plant drinks (only ~29% iodine-fortified in the German sample) and salt is cut. In Poland, using iodized salt sparingly at home and eating sea fish or eggs helps.
- **B12** is low-risk with regular meat, fish, eggs or dairy. It is a risk if animal foods are reduced, so the app should check the label for B12-fortified drinks.
- Choosing which norm to use: for a Polish app, PL norms 2020 are the natural default (RDA = covers 97.5% of people). EFSA values are a secondary reference.

### Gaps
- I could not get numeric calcium values per serving for sardines, calcium-set tofu or hard cheeses from a primary database (NIH ODS fetch returned 403). The Polish IŻŻ food composition tables are recommended for this.
- An EFSA vitamin D AI of 15 µg is widely cited, but I confirmed only the PL norm AI (15 µg) directly.
- I found no recent Polish population data on iodine status after salt reduction.

## 3. Vitamin D: Polish supplementation guidelines (2023)

### Takeaway
For healthy adults 19–65 in Poland, take **1000–2000 IU (25–50 µg)/day of D3, year-round**, adjusted for body weight and diet, unless summer sun exposure is adequate. "Adequate" means May–September, 10:00–15:00, about 18% of skin uncovered (forearms and legs), no sunscreen, for 30–45 min. In that case supplementation is "not necessary, although still recommended and safe". People with obesity need roughly double the dose.

### Cited Findings
- Adults 19–65: with adequate summer sun (30–45 min, forearms and legs uncovered, no sunscreen, 10 a.m.–3 p.m., May–September), supplementation is not necessary but still recommended and safe. Otherwise take **cholecalciferol 1000–2000 IU/day throughout the year**, depending on body weight and dietary vitamin D intake. Calcifediol 10 µg/day is a second-line option — [Płudowski et al., *Nutrients* 2023;15(3):695](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9920487/)
- Seniors 65–75: 1000–2000 IU/day year-round. Over 75: **2000–4000 IU/day year-round** — [Płudowski 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9920487/)
- Obese adults (BMI >30): usually need **about double** the dose for normal-weight peers — [Płudowski 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9920487/)
- Pregnant or lactating women: 2000 IU/day if 25(OH)D testing is unavailable. Women planning pregnancy follow the general adult recommendation — [Płudowski 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9920487/)
- Upper limits for prophylaxis: **4000 IU/day for normal-weight adults; 10,000 IU/day for adults with overweight or obesity**; 4000 IU in pregnancy and lactation. A serum 25(OH)D above 100 ng/ml is a toxicity signal — [Płudowski 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9920487/)
- 25(OH)D status cut-offs: <20 ng/ml deficiency; 20–30 suboptimal; 30–50 optimal — [Płudowski 2023](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36771403/)
- Diet alone (oily fish, cod-liver oil, fortified foods, UV-exposed mushrooms) cannot keep summer vitamin D levels through winter, but it does help — [Płudowski 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9920487/)
- PL norms 2020 dietary AI for vitamin D for adults is **15 µg/day (600 IU)** — [PL norms 2020, Table 18](https://ncez.pzh.gov.pl/wp-content/uploads/2021/03/normy_zywienia_2020web.pdf)

### Inferences
- In practice the 2023 guideline is "year-round, unless summer sun criteria are met". Many users meet the sun criteria only partly, so the app can default to a reminder for **Oct–Apr at minimum and year-round as the recommendation**, at 1000–2000 IU (2000 IU if BMI ≥30 on a weight-loss diet, up to about double the normal-weight dose).
- A user on 1500 kcal for weight loss often has BMI ≥25–30, so the higher dose band is relevant.

### Gaps
- The guideline gives ranges, not a single dose for each body weight. The app should not prescribe beyond these ranges.

## 4. Lactose intolerance: is full dairy avoidance needed? Lactose content of products

### Takeaway
No. EFSA (2010) concluded that most people with lactose maldigestion tolerate **up to 12 g lactose in a single dose** (≈ one glass of milk) with no or minor symptoms, and more when it is spread across the day. Tolerance varies, and some people react to <6 g. Hard and aged cheeses contain about 0–1 g per serving, so they are practically lactose-free. Yogurt and kefir are intermediate.

### Cited Findings
- EFSA NDA Panel (2010): "the vast majority of subjects with lactose maldigestion will tolerate up to **12 g of lactose as a single dose** with no or minor symptoms". Higher doses may be tolerated if spread over the day. No single threshold fits everyone because individual tolerance varies widely, and symptoms have been described after **<6 g** in some subjects — [EFSA Journal 2010;8(9):1777](https://www.efsa.europa.eu/efsajournal/pub/1777)
- Trade press summary of the EFSA opinion: 20–24 g spread over the day was tolerated, while 24 g taken at once usually caused symptoms and 50 g almost always did (not verified against the full text) — [DairyReporter 2010](https://www.dairyreporter.com/Article/2010/09/27/No-common-lactose-tolerability-limit-concludes-EFSA/)
- Lactose content: cow's milk is about **11–13 g per cup (~240 ml)**. Mature hard cheese has **<1 g per 43 g** portion, and alpine, Emmental, Gouda, Edam and Tilsit list **0 g per 30 g**. Yogurt and kefir vary by source, for example ~4.8 g/150 g yogurt and ~5.4 g/150 g kefir in one chart, versus 11–17 g per 6–8 oz yogurt in a Purdue guide — [Purdue Extension 2022](https://extension.purdue.edu/news/county/putnam/2022/10/rethinking-lactose-intolerance-a-guide-for-including-dairy-foods-in-the-diet.html); [MDPI *Nutrients* 2015;7(9):5340](https://mdpi.com/2072-6643/7/9/5340/htm); [Carepatron lactose chart](https://www.carepatron.com/files/lactose-food-chart.pdf) (secondary chart); [National Dairy Council](https://www.americandairy.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/01/Enjoy-Dairy-Foods-with-Confidence-Lactose-NDC.pdf) (industry source)

### Inferences
- The app can let lactose-intolerant users keep **hard and aged cheeses** (Gouda, Edam, Emmental, Parmesan-type), **lactose-free milk, yogurt and kefir** and, if tolerated, small portions of regular yogurt or kefir. This keeps dairy's calcium, iodine, B12 and protein. A useful display is "lactose per meal", with an optional soft warning above 12 g per meal.
- Yogurt figures conflict across sources (~3–12 g/100–200 g), so label-based values are preferable.

### Gaps
- I did not obtain Polish product-specific lactose values (e.g., twaróg, kefir, maślanka). Labels or the Polish composition tables are needed.

## 5. Is a multivitamin advisable at ~1500 kcal?

### Takeaway
No guideline I found sets a hard "<1600 kcal" cut-off. Reviews and modelling studies consistently show that calorie restriction lowers micronutrient intake roughly in proportion to energy, and they recommend nutrient-dense or fortified foods. A standard multivitamin/mineral (≤100% NRV) is a reasonable option for long-term restriction, particularly for menstruating women (iron) and anyone with low dairy (calcium/iodine). It does not replace the vitamin D recommendation.

### Cited Findings
- Calorie restriction "may lead to a proportional reduction in micronutrient intake unless corresponding supplementation is provided". The review notes RCTs supporting daily multivitamin/mineral supplementation during calorie restriction — [Frontiers in Nutrition 2024 review, PMC10936542](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10936542/)
- An NHANES-based modelling study of US adults with overweight/obesity found that "caloric reduction may exacerbate nutrient inadequacies". It recommends nutrient-dense foods, fortified foods, specially formulated products and/or dietary supplements for people on long-term calorie-restricted diets. Supplements lowered the share of people below the EAR for most nutrients — [PubMed 39025331 (2024)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39025331/)
- An analysis of three commercial weight-loss diet plans found gaps in essential vitamins and minerals relative to recommended intakes — [Micronutrient Gaps in Three Commercial Weight-Loss Diet Plans, PMC5793336](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC5793336/)
- A pilot in obese individuals found that a protein-rich formula diet with DRI-level micronutrients did not correct existing deficiencies. Supplementation at DRI level may not fix pre-existing deficits — [Nutrition Journal 2012;11:34](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/1475-2891-11-34)

### Inferences
- At 1500 kcal, meeting the PL RDAs for iron (women, 18 mg), calcium (1000 mg), magnesium, folate and iodine requires deliberately nutrient-dense choices. An app warning such as "after 7 days below X% of RDA for nutrient Y, consider fortified foods or a standard multivitamin/mineral, and consult a doctor or pharmacist" is defensible.
- Supplements should not exceed ULs. Vitamin D should be counted across all sources: multivitamin dose plus standalone D3 at or below 4000 IU (normal weight).

### Gaps
- I found no EFSA, NIZP-PZH or WHO statement with a specific kcal threshold such as 1200 or 1600 kcal below which a multivitamin is advised. The often-quoted "below ~1200–1600 kcal" rule appears in dietetic practice texts I did not verify.

## 6. Hydration

### Takeaway
The adequate **total** water intake (beverages + food moisture) is **2.0 L/day for women and 2.5 L/day for men**, under both EFSA and the PL norms 2020. Drinking about **500 ml of water 30 min before main meals** produced a modest extra weight loss (~1.3 kg over 12 weeks) in one RCT in adults with obesity.

### Cited Findings
- EFSA adult AI for total water: **2.5 L/day for men, 2.0 L/day for women**. This includes drinking water, all beverages and **food moisture**. Pregnancy is 2.3 L and lactation 2.7 L. Adolescents ≥14 y follow the adult values. Kidneys can excrete at most 0.7–1.0 L/hour, which is why water intoxication is possible with extreme intakes — [EFSA DRV Summary Report, Table 1 and water section](https://www.efsa.europa.eu/sites/default/files/2017_09_DRVs_summary_report.pdf)
- PL norms 2020 (AI): **men 2500 ml/day, women 2000 ml/day** across all adult age groups. This counts "water from beverages and food products". Other AIs: sodium 1500 mg, potassium 3500 mg, chloride 2300 mg — [PL norms 2020, Table 24 / Ch. Woda i elektrolity](https://ncez.pzh.gov.pl/wp-content/uploads/2021/03/normy_zywienia_2020web.pdf)
- Share of water from food: in survey data cited in the PL norms, food provided **~33% (UK) to ~40% (France)** of total water in children. A Spanish study found 58.7% came from drinks — [PL norms 2020, Ch. Woda](https://ncez.pzh.gov.pl/wp-content/uploads/2021/03/normy_zywienia_2020web.pdf)
- RCT (Parretti et al., *Obesity* 2015; Birmingham, UK; n=84 adults with obesity, primary care). Drinking **500 ml water 30 min before main meals** for 12 weeks gave **1.3 kg greater weight loss** than control (95% CI −2.4 to −0.1; P=0.028). After adjusting for confounders the difference was 1.2 kg (P=0.063), which is not significant. The authors called the evidence preliminary — [PubMed 26237305](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pubmed/26237305); [CORE record](https://core.ac.uk/works/78424878)
- Press reports of the same trial say those who preloaded before all 3 meals lost 4.3 kg on average (subgroup, secondary reporting) — [FoodNavigator 2015](https://www.foodnavigator.com/Article/2015/09/09/weight-loss-water-drinking-before-each-meal-aids-weight-reduction/)

### Inferences
- Assuming food supplies about 20–30% of water in adults (an approximation; see Gaps), a target for **drinks** of about **1.5 L for women and 1.9–2.0 L for men** is reasonable. On a reduced-energy diet, food volume and food water are lower, so drinks may need to make up a larger share.
- A pre-meal water reminder ("a glass or two, ~500 ml, 30 min before a main meal") is low-risk and supported by modest RCT evidence. It should be presented as a helpful habit, not a proven weight-loss treatment.

### Gaps
- I could not confirm the commonly quoted "20–30% of water comes from food" for adults directly in the EFSA summary text. It probably appears in the full EFSA 2010 water opinion (EFSA Journal 2010;8(3):1459), which I did not read.
- I did not retrieve the Dennis et al. 2010 (older adults, *Obesity*) water-preload trial, so it is not cited here.
